# The verification round

**There is no zeroth rung.** Every answer gets at least the cheap round, so the question isn't *"does
this deserve verification?"* but **"how big?"**.

## How you weigh it

Three quantities: the **harm** · **how binding the answer stays** · the **complexity** (how many
things must be true at once).

| Rule | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Who weighs it** | **the agent**, as a hypothesis declared in one line. The owner corrects it in one line |
| **Raising it mid-flight** | **needs no reason**, at the first thing the weight didn't anticipate |
| **Lowering it** | ⚠️ **only with a written reason.** Same rule as overturning a rejection: without it you end up switching the control off instead of calibrating it |
| **The cost** | **declared on delivery.** It's the only antidote to ceremony |

## The five rungs

1. **cheap round** — back to the line on every citation. **Always, on everything.**
2. **one refuter**
3. **refuter + a check on the remedy**
4. **the full chain until a round comes back empty**
5. for **unattended** work, ⚠️ **the round IS the mechanism**: not an extra, it's the chain itself.

⚠️ **And every rung, before it closes, rereads the frozen decisions.** It's the last act of whichever rung
you're on, never a sixth rung. **You open the frozen decisions the round touched and check whether
their reopening condition has come true**: a newer model, a more recent source, a fact that contradicts
the measurement they rested on. If one has, **the block reopens and you write that down**, ⚠️ **and
reopening means citing the closure you're reopening**: when it was closed, on what grounds, and **what
has changed since**. Without that citation a reopening is indistinguishable from a fresh decision, which
is exactly the shape of **silent change**: nobody lies, the old version simply **disappears and nobody
is left aware it was ever there**. If none has,
**you declare they were reread**. ⚠️ **Without this act the rule stays written and no step executes it**,
which is the defect this very page teaches you to spot.

⚠️ **Only one of the five is measured** (all three quantities high → 5 rounds). The other four
calibrate by being used, and until they are, say so.

## The three scales, when an agent works on itself

| Scale | Shape | When |
|---|---|---|
| **single** | produce; then **a clean context rereads only the artifact and the rubric**, without the reasoning | low harm, reversible, no decision others inherit |
| **pair** | one builds, another is mandated to **demolish**, not to confirm | **the default** above the cheap rung |
| **team** | fan-out by surface + completeness + refutation | the work splits into independent pieces |

⚠️ **The declared failure mode of this rule: an agent that asks itself questions tends to ask THE ONES
IT ALREADY HAS THE ANSWER TO.** The antidote isn't good intentions, it's structural: the judge doesn't
see the reasoning, and **at least one round is mandated to ATTACK**. ⚠️ **And a round that confirms
DOES NOT COUNT AS A ROUND**: it isn't a successful round with a good outcome, it's **a round that
didn't happen** — you redo it with the mandate to demolish, and until it is redone the delivery declares
**one round fewer** than it appears to have. Without this line *"two rounds, both confirm"* reads as
evidence, and that is the most comfortable shape of the round that never took place.

⚠️ **Step 0 on yourself**: before producing, answer **in writing** the owner's questions applied to
your own task. Two lines, not a page. It's a **declared deduction**, so it goes through the round like
everything else.

⚠️ **Team size is a prescription, not a habit**: start at **one**, grow to **three**, and **five is
the ceiling**. *"Everyone on the team does everything"*, and coordination is talking to the person on
your left and your right. Past that size, coordination becomes a job of its own.

⚠️ **And a small thing that's worth more than its weight**: **give every session a name and a colour**.
When four agents finish and go quiet, the first problem is
**telling which session is which**, before you even work out what went wrong.

## The rules this round earned in the field

1. ⚠️ **The second round doesn't repeat the first: it attacks it.** A verifier that confirms adds no
   information; one that must **demolish** does, even when it fails. ⚠️ **And it doesn't count as a
   round**: you redo it with the mandate reversed, and you don't write it into the delivery as though
   it had happened.
2. ⚠️ **Whoever acquits gets checked as hard as whoever accuses.** Measured: a defender was right about
   the method and abused it: **nine times out of ten it erased what should have been reduced**.
3. ⚠️ **You stop when a round comes back empty**, not when you're tired.
4. ⚠️ **A round that INVENTS work to justify itself is worse than a round that doesn't happen.** Write
   it into the verifier's mandate: *"don't invent work: if it's dry, say so."*
5. ⚠️ **After a remedy, the next round doesn't look at the object: it looks at the lines the remedy
   wrote.** Measured: **seven errors out of seven introduced by the correction**, with one signature:
   *you fix the accused cell and break the sentence next to it*. Nobody looks, because **whoever just
   corrected feels fine**.
6. ⚠️ **Where there's a citation, go back to the line. Always.** It caught **nine errors out of nine**.
   The typical failure here is the **half-quotation**: a real sentence, cut or glossed, producing a
   false and credible claim, and it showed up **at every layer**: in the accusations, in the
   acquittals, and in the corrections.
7. ⚠️ **Five mentions of a premise are not five statements of the same invariant.** That's the
   half-quotation moved inside a **count**, and it's harder to see than a cut sentence.
8. ⚠️ **Before believing a number, ask HOW MUCH the tool that produced it LOOKS AT.** A tool sees a
   slice, and whoever uses it promotes their own narrow view to a **property of the world**: it isn't a
   wrong number, it's a **deduced absence**. Four cases in two days — a regex that saw **one form of
   `require` out of three** (16 places instead of 21), a denominator counting as *output* only the
   outputs **already judged** (100% instead of 54%), a declared age that wasn't computable because the
   instant it needed lived **in a file the script had written for itself**. ⚠️ **And the datum that
   says where the cure is**: all four times it was found by **the refuter, never by whoever was
   measuring** — 4 out of 4. Whoever reads a number produced by a tool writes down **what that tool
   does not look at**; if they don't know, that is the first finding.

## When a round goes wrong, look at the briefing first

⚠️ **The defect is almost always in the protocol, not in whoever executed it.** Measured: a labelling
round produced a category inflated **by 67%**, and the cause wasn't the agents but one line of the
protocol instructing them to treat as findings some sections that **judge nothing**. Another time a
briefing said *"this is the reference case for false positives"* and moved one label in twenty-five.

**The signature you recognise it by**: the agents **all get it wrong the same way**. An error
distributed evenly comes from **one wrong line upstream**, not from many people being careless, and
that's where to look before the verdicts.

## The mandate of whoever you delegate to

**A mandate declares how many outputs it produces and in what format. Never a goal, never "at least N".**

| Rule | In one line |
|---|---|
| **How it's written** | number of outputs + format: `12 numbered verdicts`, never *"sample about 20"* |
| **Why** | *at least* is open-ended upward: **there is no point at which the agent can say "I'm done"**. Measured: countable mandate **1,387 verdicts in 15 minutes**; open mandate **0 bytes in 57 minutes** |
| **Who enumerates** | ⚠️ **whoever dispatches.** If the work can't be counted, they prepare the closed list **first**: you don't delegate the counting |
| **The alarm** | **~15 minutes with no byte on disk**: retrim the mandate, don't wait |
| **Who watches it** | ⚠️ **whoever dispatched, and it isn't obvious: it's the one threshold of the method nobody was watching.** Whoever dispatches **stays listening** (a check that expires against the delivery file, not a mental reminder), and when it expires they **retrim the mandate**, they don't ask the agent how it's going. An agent stuck on a question nobody can see **declares itself "available"**: its word is not a signal |
| **And the alarm only works if the mandate allows it** | ⚠️ *"delivery is a file"* **isn't enough on its own**: if the file is written at the end, *"zero bytes"* is the **normal** state for the whole run, and the threshold can't tell *working* from *dead*. The mandate must require **writing as it goes** (one line per output, as soon as there is one), or the alarm is blind in exactly the case it exists for |
| **Necessary, not sufficient** | ⚠️ a third agent with **12 numbered items** still returned **0 bytes in 31 minutes**. Below a certain size **delegating costs more than doing** |
| **Delivery** | **is a file.** A message can fail to arrive without **either side** knowing |
| **Where it matters most** | ⚠️ **on refuters.** *"Attack this"* is the task that widens by itself (there's no point at which you've finished attacking), and it is precisely the one that went silent: **0 bytes in 57 minutes**. You send a refuter with **a number of accusations and their format**, like everyone else |

⚠️ **The four ways of going quiet, and none looks like a fault**: *it asked something of nobody* (stuck
on a prompt) · *it delivered to nobody* (finished, message never arrived) · *it never finishes*
(open-ended mandate) · *it always exits 0* (cannot fail by construction).

## The critic: the refuter with the mandate reversed

⚠️ **It fires on a BLOCKER, not on an artifact.** When somebody declares a limit (*"it can't be
done"*) and the blocker **survived the three lines** (exact error text · counterexample looked for ·
what would have to be true), you dispatch the critic.

**Not a new agent: the refuter, with three things reversed.**

| | **Refuter on an artifact** | **Critic on a blocker** |
|---|---|---|
| **When it starts** | after delivery | ⚠️ **when somebody declares a limit** |
| **What it attacks** | *this is true* | *this is impossible* |
| **What it sees** | only the artifact, **never the reasoning** | ⚠️ **all the work done** |

⚠️ **That last row reverses, and it isn't a slip.** On a finished artifact, not seeing the reasoning is
**the defence** against self-absolution. On a blocker it's **blindness**: the counterexample that
demolishes an *"it can't be done"* lives **precisely inside the work already done**, usually in the
same session.

⚠️ **Why not a separate agent**: one more costs its description in **every** session, including the
days nobody is blocked. What was missing was never a new craft: it was **the trigger**.

⚠️ **And most blockers never get here**: they die at the three lines, which cost nothing. The critic
is the second rung, not the first.

## Reopening conditions are a step of this round

**Every frozen decision carries the condition that would reopen it**, and the conditions **get checked
here**, as a declared step, not when somebody remembers.

| Kind | |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **a new model** | **applies to ALL of them, and isn't up for debate**: a better model turns old instructions into bugs, and the defect **looks like the model's** |
| **a more recent source** | what overturns most often, and you date it **by the models it names** |
| **a number that moves** | the measurement that authorised the decision, re-run |
| **a real case the decision didn't anticipate** | the overturn |

⚠️ **What is NOT a condition**: *"if we realise it's wrong"*. That's the status quo with a sentence
added.

⚠️ **And one case no written condition can catch, which is worth saying instead of pretending
otherwise**: when **the owner changes their mind** and no fact changed. Measured: of fifteen real
reopenings, the conditions caught ten, and among the five missed was exactly that one.

⚠️ **And the underlying tension is DECLARED, not resolved.** This method prescribes **frozen blocks** —
a decision taken, written down, not touched without reopening it — while the sources much of its
material comes from say of themselves that this is *"an art, as things stand"* and *"I don't have a
science to give you"*. That isn't a difference of tone: if the field is an art, a frozen block is a
posture dressed up as a measurement. Here you **don't choose between the two**: you keep the freezing,
because it prevents the **silent change** this method exists for; and you keep beside it the declaration
that **what is frozen is not a science** — that the reopening condition isn't a formality at the foot of
the block, it's the part that matters. ⚠️ **A block read without this line looks like a result**; read
with it, it is what it is: the best anyone knew that day.

## `neatnik:tidy` — the check you run BEFORE saying "ready"

**The question it answers**: *is everything we saw, decided and reviewed actually in there?* ⚠️ **Not
an academic question, it came out of a failure**: work declared ready with **a fifth** of what had
been decided actually inside it, and the owner is the one who found that out. The defence hadn't
failed through carelessness: **no check existed that could fail** on a decision left on paper.

⚠️ **The word "ready" isn't used without a passed `tidy`.** If it hasn't run, the delivery says
**"work finished, not yet verified"**, a different and honest sentence. This isn't advice: advice is
exactly what existed on the day of the failure, and nobody followed it.

**What gets counted.** The unit is the **decision**; the **ticket inherits the worst box** of its own.
That way *"is this ticket finished?"* has a flat answer instead of an average: a ticket with three of
four things done doesn't come back as *"done"*.

**Where the list comes from.** From **written** decisions: the map, the tickets, the project's
documents. Past contexts and histories are there to **notice** what was decided and never written
down, ⚠️ **but whatever surfaces that way is a candidate marked NOT CONFIRMED and doesn't enter the
count**: a check that builds its own list of what it must verify can always declare itself fine. And a
decision never written **doesn't count and is declared unread**, and the cure is upstream, in writing it
down at the end of the session.

### The three levels

⚠️ **Coverage is NEVER cut — but it is NOT REPEATED, and those are two different things.** The
**first** pass reads every decision. From the second on you read **the delta**, which is two things
added together: **(a)** what **fell** at the last attack or stayed open · **(b)** what was **touched**
after that pass had already read it — and the pass finds that for itself, with a `find -newermt`,
because a hand-written list forgets the files the remedy changed without saying so. ⚠️ **Without (b),
every correction made after the last read would enter the count as "already verified"**: the very
defect the round exists to find, committed by the round itself.

⚠️ **The reason is measured, and for twelve passes I confused the two**: re-reading everything every
time, by the twelfth pass you were paying to re-verify **433 decisions confirmed by the first**, at
**1.15 million tokens per pass**. The delta typically brings that down to **thirty or forty items**.

⚠️ **And the delta applies to ALL THREE agents, not just the reader.** Giving it to the reader and
leaving the attacker and the remedy to read everything removes the cost from **one third** of the round
and reports it as the cost of the round — measured: the reader dropped **79%**, the other two by
**zero**. How they narrow:

| | what it receives |
|---|---|
| **the attacker** | the boxes from **this** pass, plus **8 old confirmations** picked at random. ⚠️ The fixed handful isn't zeal: a box confirmed at pass 1 **stops being true** if a later pass's remedy moved the line that proved it, and without those 8 nobody would ever look at it again. Fixed, because a count that changes makes the rates incomparable |
| **the remedy** | only the items **still open**. ⚠️ Whatever was declared *"a choice"* **leaves the round at the first pass**: it belongs to the owner, and keeping it in doesn't move it forward — but it **inflates the count of open items**, so the stopping threshold never fires because of things waiting on them |
| **the holes** | attacked at the **first** pass and at the **last**, not every round. First, because if a third of the holes aren't holes the remedy spends three passes chasing work that doesn't exist. Last, because that's the certification |

⚠️ **And every pass leaves a summary for the next one, instead of making it rebuild everything** — with
a limit that has to be written down or the remedy is worse than the disease: **the summary says WHERE to
look, never WHAT to conclude.** It's the word of whoever wrote it, and this round exists because that
word isn't enough: whoever reads reopens the `file:line` anyway. **A summary that replaces the reading is
the defect we're hunting, served on a plate.**

⚠️ **And the delta is read by ONE agent, not four**: slices are for splitting a large corpus, and the
delta is not large. Four contexts for thirty items are three contexts paid for and unused.

That said: **all three levels read every decision** on the first pass. What changes is how much gets
attacked and how many times you go round. **A level that looks at less is more dishonest, not cheaper**:
*"I didn't look at it"* and *"I looked and it was fine"* resemble each other far too much.

| | Attack | Stops when |
|---|---|---|
| **`:low`** | reopens full boxes **until 5 in a row hold**, floor **8**, ceiling **20** | one pass only. ⚠️ **It may not say "complete"** |
| **`:medium`** | reopens **every** full box | no pass finds anything under 100% any more |
| **`:max`** | every full box **plus the holes**: does what's declared missing exist somewhere? | **two passes in a row** with nothing blocking and the attack **under 10%** |

⚠️ **"Entirely empty-handed" was the rule, and it was unreachable by construction.** The attacker
**always** finds something non-blocking: that's its trade. Measured: **twelve passes without ever
stopping**, and what stopped the round was the ceiling, not the cleanliness. A threshold nobody can
reach isn't rigour — it's a round that never ends, and that always ends the worst way.

⚠️ **And a rate is ALWAYS read with its denominator.** "42%" on 73 boxes reopened and "12%" on 426
**do not belong in the same column**: lining them up tells a curve that doesn't exist. Both numbers go
in the delivery, never the percentage alone.

⚠️ **Why a sample with a variable stop and not a fixed number.** A fixed number decides when to stop
**without looking at what it's finding**: it halts at eight one step before the big one, or digs to
twenty inventing work. Here the condition for stopping is **finding clean**, never *having found
enough*, so **digging doesn't pay**. And the ceiling doesn't limit the search: when it's hit without
ever seeing 5 clean in a row, the round declares *"this count is too inflated for a low level"* and
**escalates by itself**.

**`blocking`** = any decision **not at 100%**, missing or half-there. What the attacker cut down to
size but which still stands is not blocking.

⚠️ **A small round that finds something big raises itself on the spot, without asking**, and writes
that it did. **Lowering**, by contrast, requires a written line of reason. Same asymmetry as overturning
a rejection, for the same reason: without it you end up switching the check off instead of calibrating it.

**Who picks the level.** Neatnik proposes it in one line, the owner corrects it in one line; **the
written word always wins**. With no word it runs **`:medium`**, ⚠️ and the default matters: the low one
would put inflated numbers into circulation, the top one would make you wait half an hour for a
two-minute question.

**What it fixes.** What is **mechanical** it closes, then **re-runs to show it closed**; what is **a
choice** becomes one line for the owner.

⚠️ **And the remedy sits INSIDE the round, between one pass and the next — not after.** Found on round
11, while `tidy` was running: the tool was re-reading **an unchanged artifact**, so the next pass found
the same holes again and **the cycle could not converge**. The prose said *"fix it and re-run"*, and no
phase did it: the usual signature, this time inside the tool that exists to find it.

**The line that divides the two**: **mechanical** is what has only one right form — a pointer to the
wrong file, a number that doesn't match its own list, a rule with no step that executes it, two lines
saying different things about the same fact. **A choice** is what has more than one defensible form:
removing or keeping a rule, changing a threshold, overturning a decision of the owner's. ⚠️ **When in
doubt it's a choice**: a wrong mechanical remedy enters in silence and stays there. ⚠️ The law
*"you don't fix"* belongs to the **judge**, which must not be able to repair what it is judging.
**`tidy` isn't the judge**, and the round has always had *refuter + a check on the remedy* inside it.

### The tools, and they ship

| | |
|---|---|
| `tools/tidy.js` | the round: reads, attacks, re-runs, escalates by itself |
| `tools/inventory.py` | rebuilds the count from the agents' files. ⚠️ It hunts **the box**, not the shape, and exits **1** if a box it saw ends up in no decision |
| `tools/coverage.py` | exits **1** if a decision isn't at 100%, **2 if the inventory is too small** |
| `tools/history.py` | one line per round, and the **curve** |
| `tools/citations.py` | the first rung: does every `file:line` cited actually exist? |
| `tools/images.py` | the check behind `R-01`: measures images instead of looking at them |

⚠️ **The history is the only thing that tells you whether you're converging or just writing, and it
isn't bookkeeping.** Nine consecutive rounds on this very method showed that, and the sequence lived only in
the memory of whoever was there: the curve was **written into the method as a thing to look at** and
**no step wrote it down**.

### The shape of the round

⚠️ **It's the verification round applied to an artifact nobody can hold in their head all at once**:
a skill, a method, a system. Not an extra scale: it's the **pair** and the **team**, mounted on
something with hundreds of lines to check instead of eight.

**The shape, and every piece answers a measured defect:**

| Step | What | Why that step exists |
|---|---|---|
| **1. start FROM THE DECISION** | one slice of decisions per agent, and for each you search **in the artifact** | ⚠️ The opposite direction (reading the artifact and looking for its decision) **only finds what's there**. It's the defect one source calls a *saturated eval* |
| **2. three boxes, not two** | `IMPLEMENTED` · `PARTIAL` · `ABSENT`, plus **out of scope** for what shouldn't have entered | Without the middle box everything becomes *"it's there"*, and **the dominant defect lives exactly there** |
| **3. ATTACK the full boxes** | a refuter reopens the `IMPLEMENTED` ones, goes to the `file:line` and asks: does that line **put the decision into practice**, or name the concept? | ⚠️ `IMPLEMENTED` **closes the search**. Measured: of 42 boxes reopened, **33 didn't hold** |
| **4. attack the HOLES too** | does what the check declares absent exist somewhere? | Measured: **9 holes out of 22 weren't holes.** Both extremes are inflated, in opposite directions |
| **5. close it, and RUN IT AGAIN** | and watch **the defect-rate curve**, not just the count | It's the curve that tells you whether you're converging or just writing |

**How to read the curve, and it's counterintuitive.** The defect rate **can rise while the object
improves**: once the big holes are closed, the attacker stops looking for *"missing"* and starts
looking for **"declared with no executor"**, which is harder to find and easier to get wrong.
⚠️ **A rate rising on an improving object is an attacker who raised their aim, not work getting
worse.** What must fall is **severity**: first whole decisions collapse, then only downgrades.

**The defect that survives longest, and you hunt it by name:**

⚠️ **THE RULE IS WRITTEN AND NO STEP EXECUTES IT.** It survives every round because `grep` finds it:
the line is there, it says the right thing, and **it has no subject**. The tell: a file **no step
opens**, a line that says *what must be true* without saying **who does it and when**, a pointer to a
section that doesn't describe what it promises.
**The cure is giving the existing lines an executor, never adding more of them.**

**Four things this round learned about itself, and they carry to the next:**

1. ⚠️ **The mandate must say WRITE AS YOU GO.** *"Delivery is a file"* isn't enough on its own: if the
   file appears at the end, *"zero bytes"* is the normal state for the whole run and the fifteen-minute
   threshold **can no longer tell "working" from "dead"**.
2. ⚠️ **The check must not be able to say OK on zero data.** An empty inventory **isn't clean**: it's a
   check that read nothing, and it's the worst false ok there is.
3. ⚠️ **Agents write in different shapes every round.** A reader that chases formats loses: hunt **the
   box** and attribute it to the last numbered heading.
4. ⚠️ **After a remedy, the round looks at the NEW lines.** Measured twice on this very object: a
   correction applied **in one place out of five**, with the other four still saying the old thing,
   and two checks citing them as evidence **without seeing the contradiction**.

⚠️ **Every round writes into a folder of its own, and this isn't fussiness.** A round's files have the
same names as the previous round's; left in the same place they **end up inside the new round** and the
attacker demolishes them as if they were its own. Measured: at one pass 2 the attacker reopened **five**
checks, and **four were leftovers from the previous round**. It raises no error — it produces an attack
on dead material, and the rate that comes out doesn't measure the object being run.

⚠️ **And the remedy has to know WHICH COPY to write on.** A method often lives in two places — where it
is written and where it is executed — and those are different files with the same content. Measured: a
remedy wrote its correction **only into the copy in service**, and it wasn't in the repository: without
somebody noticing, the first synchronisation would have erased it. The mandate lists the paths in full,
and whatever it finds in two copies **it declares**.

⚠️ **The artifact is NOT touched while the round is reading it, and that was expensive to learn.** On
round 11 I kept correcting while the slices were reading: **the `file:line` pointers shifted under the
feet of the people verifying them**, and the attacker produced whole blocks of off-by-one findings —
true as observations, **useless as work**. The defect rate climbed from 4% to 15.7% and almost all of
that jump was noise I had produced myself.

**The rule**: during a round the artifact is **frozen**. What the round finds gets closed **between one
pass and the next**, which is the place the remedy phase exists to occupy — not while it reads.
⚠️ **And if freezing it isn't possible**, the round declares it on delivery: *"the artifact changed
during the check, the pointers hold for the HH:MM version"*. A delivery that doesn't say so looks in
every way like a delivery on a still object.

⚠️ **When it stops**: when a round comes back **empty**, not when the count looks good. And a round
that **invents work** to justify itself is worse than a round that doesn't happen.

## What you don't delegate

⚠️ **Conversations with the owner.** An agent answering its own questions is not a conversation. You
delegate the **measurable half**; the line of choice stays theirs and **is written before the
measurement**, not after.

⚠️ **And the failure mode of whoever decides what to delegate**: they tend to keep the **posture**
calls too, which are only apparently theirs. The criterion: **if it can be counted, count it; if it's
a posture, it's the owner's**, and no count substitutes for it.

## What gets declared on delivery, or the round didn't happen

**The scale used · what the judge saw · how many accusations and how many held · the cost · which frozen
decisions were reread, and whether any reopened.**
